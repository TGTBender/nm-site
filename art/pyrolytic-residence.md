# Pyrolytic Residence

*An algorithmic philosophy*

---

## The movement

**Pyrolytic Residence** concerns itself with a single, under-examined idea: that
the most permanent structures in nature are not built, but *converted*. Fire
applied without oxygen does not consume — it transmutes. What enters as living
tissue leaves as lattice. The movement takes its name from two terms of art:
*pyrolysis*, the conversion itself, and *residence time*, the span across which
the converted thing persists. Both are measurements. Neither is poetry. That is
precisely the point.

This is not a philosophy of growth, nor of decay. It is a philosophy of the
narrow band between them — the threshold at which a system stops being one kind
of thing and becomes another, and of what survives that crossing. The algorithm
must locate this threshold and make it visible, and the locating must be the
work of an implementation refined through countless iterations by someone
operating at the absolute top of their field.

## The computational thesis

Every generative system in this movement is organised around a **transformation
front** — a boundary, never perfectly straight, that divides the field into a
before and an after. Agents originate on one side and are obligated to cross.
The crossing is irreversible. What distinguishes Pyrolytic Residence from the
countless flow-field studies that preceded it is that agents do not merely
*move* through the field; they are **changed by their position within it**.
Velocity, width, opacity, chromatic identity, and branching disposition are all
functions of depth relative to the front. An agent at rest above the boundary
and the same agent below it are not the same agent.

Above the front: **vascular behaviour**. Filaments that branch, taper, reach,
and seek — thin, luminous, structurally ambitious. Their geometry should recall
conductive tissue: channels that carry something. Below the front: **lattice
behaviour**. The same filaments thicken, lose their fineness, spread laterally,
and begin depositing voids. They stop reaching and start *staying*. The
transition between these two regimes must never be abrupt. It is a gradient,
noise-perturbed along its length so that no two horizontal positions cross at
the same depth, and the gradient's tuning is where a master-level implementation
distinguishes itself from a competent one.

## Emergence and the ember

At the front itself, briefly, there is heat. Not flame — this is oxygen-starved
conversion — but the incandescence of matter reorganising. The algorithm should
express this as a narrow chromatic event: a warmth that appears only within the
transformation band and nowhere else, never applied as decoration but arising
strictly as a function of an agent's depth. Its intensity must be earned through
painstaking calibration. Too much and the piece becomes a sunset; too little and
the central thesis of the movement goes unstated. The correct value lives in a
narrow interval that only iteration will find.

What emerges below is not designed but **accumulated**. Thousands of
deposits, each individually trivial, aggregating into strata. Horizontal
banding should appear without being drawn — a consequence of lateral spreading
under a noise field, not a rendered feature. The voids likewise: porosity is the
defining physical property of the converted material, and it must arise as a
behaviour of the agents rather than as a texture applied afterward. A system in
which the pores are stamped on is a system that has misunderstood itself.

## On composition and restraint

This movement rejects the centred, symmetrical, evenly-weighted composition
outright. Density must be **biased** — gathered toward one region, thinning to
near-silence in another, so that the field has somewhere to breathe and
somewhere to speak. The quiet region is not empty space; it is the negative
mass that makes the dense region legible. A meticulously crafted implementation
treats this bias as a first-class parameter, not an accident of seeding.

Restraint governs the palette absolutely. Five values, no more: the dark ground
into which everything settles, the living green of what has not yet crossed, the
brief warmth of crossing, the earth-brown of what has, and a single pale value
held in reserve for the finest filaments only. Colour is never chosen — it is
*derived*, always, from an agent's depth and velocity. Any implementation in
which a colour is picked rather than computed has failed the philosophy.

## The demand

Each seed must produce a field that could not have been drawn by hand and could
not have been arrived at twice. Reproducibility is absolute — the same seed
yields the same field, always — but the space between seeds must be genuinely
generous, revealing different facets of the same underlying physics rather than
cosmetic reshuffles of one composition. This is the mark of deep computational
expertise: a parameter space in which every region is worth visiting.

The finished algorithm should read as the product of long refinement — every
threshold interrogated, every ratio deliberate, every default the survivor of
many rejected alternatives. Nothing arbitrary. Nothing left at its first
plausible value. Beauty here is not in any single frame but in the process that
produces all of them, and that process must be built with the patience of
someone who has done this many times before.
